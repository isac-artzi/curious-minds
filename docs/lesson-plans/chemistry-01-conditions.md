# Conditions Change Everything

**Grades 6–9 · 50 minutes · 🧪 Chemistry What-If Lab**

**Standards:** MS-PS1-2, MS-PS1-4 · SEP 2, 6 · CCC Cause and Effect

## Objective
Students will explain that the products of a chemical reaction depend on the
*conditions* under which it occurs, not on the reactants alone.

## The misconception this targets
Most students believe a set of chemicals has one fixed outcome — "hydrogen plus
oxygen makes water" as a permanent fact. Conditions are treated as background
detail. This lesson breaks that in about ten minutes.

## Materials
- One device per pair (Chromebook fine), the Curious Minds URL
- Prediction sheet: three rows — *What I predict* / *What happened* / *Why I was wrong*

## Procedure

**Hook (5 min).** Ask: "If I give two chemists the same two chemicals, do they
always get the same thing?" Take a hand vote. Record the count on the board —
you will return to it.

**Model together (10 min).** Project the lab. Turn on **🎯 Challenge mode**.
Select hydrogen and oxygen, 500 K, catalyst `spark`. Have the class predict
aloud, then reveal. Water. Confirm the balanced equation together — count atoms
on both sides.

**The turn (10 min).** Keep the *identical* elements. Drag temperature to
**10,000 K**. Before revealing, ask for a show of hands: still water? Most will
say yes.

It isn't. At plasma temperatures molecules cannot hold together. Let the surprise
sit for a moment before explaining — the confusion is doing the work.

**Pairs explore (15 min).** Each pair tests one variable while holding others
fixed:
- Pair A: vary temperature only (10 K / 500 K / 5,000 K)
- Pair B: vary pressure only (0.001 / 1 / 1,000 atm)
- Pair C: vary catalyst only (none / `spark` / `platinum`)

Each pair records what changed and what stayed the same.

**Share-out (10 min).** One sentence per pair: "When we changed ___, ___
happened." Build a class list on the board. Return to the opening vote and ask
whether anyone has changed their answer.

## Assessment
**Exit ticket:** *"Two students combine the same elements and get different
products. Give two reasons this could happen — without either of them making a
mistake."*

Look for: temperature, pressure, catalyst, or quantity. Two distinct condition
variables earns full credit.

## Differentiation
- **Support:** give pairs a preset `.curious` file so setup isn't a barrier.
- **Extension:** switch **Mode** to `speculative` and try a combination with no
  documented answer. What does the app do when it doesn't know? (It says so.)

## Homework
Find a real-world reaction that depends on conditions — bread rising, food
spoiling, iron rusting, a firework. Explain which condition matters and why.
