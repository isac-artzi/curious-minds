# Auditing the Machine

**Grades 9–12 · 50 minutes · 🧪 Chemistry What-If Lab**

**Standards:** HS-PS1-2 · CSTA 3A-IC-24, 3A-IC-25 · SEP 7, 8

## Objective
Students will evaluate AI-generated scientific claims by confidence tier and
construct an evidence-based argument for or against a specific claim.

## Why this lesson matters
Your students already use AI. Almost none of them have been taught to interrogate
its output. This lesson uses chemistry as the vehicle for the more durable skill.

## Materials
- One device per student
- Access to a textbook or reliable reference for verification
- Claim-audit sheet: *Claim* / *Confidence tier* / *My verdict* / *Evidence*

## Procedure

**Frame (5 min).** "This app labels every claim as well-documented, probable, or
speculative. Today your job is to decide whether it labeled honestly." Note the
stakes: you are not checking whether it's *smart*, you're checking whether it's
*calibrated*.

**Establish the baseline (10 min).** Together, run sodium + chlorine at 298 K.
Nearly everything comes back 🟢 *well_documented* — and it should. Verify two
claims against the textbook. Establish that the green tier is trustworthy.

**Push it (10 min).** Switch **Mode** to `speculative`. Run something exotic —
uranium + helium at 10,000 K, or an audience suggestion. Watch tiers shift to 🟡
and ⚪. Ask: *what changed?* Not the app — the **question**.

**Independent audit (15 min).** Each student runs their own combination, selects
**one 🟡 probable or ⚪ speculative claim**, and completes the audit sheet.
Requirement: cite one outside source, and reach a verdict — *supported*,
*contradicted*, or *cannot determine*.

"Cannot determine" is a legitimate and often correct answer. Say so explicitly,
or students will manufacture false certainty.

**Socratic close (10 min).** Work the room:
- Did anyone find something labeled well-documented that they could *not* verify?
- Did anyone find a speculative claim that turned out well-supported?
- **Is a confident wrong answer worse than an uncertain right one?**
- What would this app be like with no confidence labels at all?

## Assessment
**Written paragraph:** state the claim you audited, its tier, your verdict, your
evidence, and whether you think the labeling was honest.

Rubric: identifies the claim precisely (2) · cites real evidence (3) · reaches a
defensible verdict (3) · evaluates the labeling itself (2).

## Extension
Open `data/chemistry/elements.json` on the projector. Show that the model is
*reading from a file*, not recalling from memory. Ask what would happen if a
value in that file were wrong — and who would notice.

That question is the whole AI-literacy curriculum in one sentence.
