# Finding Goldilocks

**Grades 5–8 · 50 minutes · 🪐 Planet / Exoplanet Builder**

**Standards:** MS-ESS1-3 · SEP 2, 4 · CCC Scale, Proportion, and Quantity

## Objective
Students will determine, through systematic testing, that liquid water requires a
specific range of distances from a star — and that this range depends on the star.

## Materials
- One device per pair
- Number line, 0 to 3 AU, for marking findings

## Procedure

**Ground the unit (5 min).** Define AU: Earth-to-Sun = 1 AU. Mark Earth on the
class number line. Ask where Mars goes (1.5) and Venus (0.72).

**Build Earth (10 min).** Together: star **Sun**, distance **1.0 AU**, mass
**1.0** Earth, atmosphere **nitrogen–oxygen**, water **70%**. Run it. Habitable —
of course. Establish this as the control.

**Move it (10 min).** Turn on **🎯 Challenge mode**. Change *only* distance:
- **0.3 AU** — predict first. (Too hot. Water boils away.)
- **2.0 AU** — predict first. (Too cold. Frozen.)

Ask what stayed the same: everything except distance. That's a controlled
experiment, and you should name it as one.

**Pairs find the edges (15 min).** Each pair narrows down the *closest* and
*farthest* distance that still supports liquid water, testing systematically.
They mark both on the number line.

Encourage halving the interval — if 0.8 works and 0.5 doesn't, try 0.65. That's
binary search, and fifth graders do it naturally once prompted.

**The twist (10 min).** Switch the star to **Proxima Centauri** — a red dwarf,
much dimmer. Re-run at 1.0 AU. Frozen solid.

Now find the zone for *this* star. It sits around 0.05 AU — closer than Mercury
is to our Sun. Ask: **why does a dimmer star have a closer habitable zone?**

## Assessment
**Number line + two sentences:** mark the habitable zone for both stars, and
explain in your own words why they're different.

Look for the idea that the zone depends on how much energy the star emits — not
on distance alone.

## Differentiation
- **Support:** give the 0.3 and 2.0 endpoints; students find only the boundaries.
- **Extension:** three stars, three zones, plotted together. Ask what the pattern
  predicts for a star *hotter* than the Sun.

## The wonder moment
End here: **every real exoplanet in this app was discovered by an actual
telescope.** Proxima Centauri b is real, 4.2 light-years away, and sits in its
star's habitable zone. We found it. Students tend to assume this is all
hypothetical; it isn't, and telling them so lands.
