# The Experiment That Broke Classical Physics

**Grades 10–12 · 50 minutes · 🔬 Physics Lab**

**Standards:** HS-PS4-3, HS-PS4-4 · SEP 6, 7 · CCC Cause and Effect, Energy and Matter

## Objective
Students will evaluate wave and particle models of light against photoelectric
data and construct an argument for why intensity cannot substitute for frequency.

## The stakes
This is the experiment that forced physics to accept that light is quantized. It
won Einstein the Nobel Prize — **not** relativity. It's also one of the rare
places where students can watch a theory *fail* on evidence.

## Materials
- One device per student
- Two-column comparison sheet: *Wave model predicts* / *What actually happens*

## Procedure

**Set the trap (10 min).** Teach the wave model as if it's correct, because for a
century it was. Light is a wave; a wave carries energy; more intense light carries
more energy. Therefore: **shine bright enough light of any color on a metal and
electrons will eventually be knocked loose.**

Have students write that prediction down and commit to it. The commitment matters.

**Test it (10 min).** Load **💡 Just below threshold**. Turn up the intensity. And
again. Nothing. No electrons, at any brightness, ever.

Sit with the failure. Don't rescue it. The wave model just made a clear
prediction and reality declined.

**The contrast (10 min).** Load **💜 UV pops electrons**. Dim, faint UV — far less
total energy than the bright red — and electrons emit *instantly*.

The comparison sheet fills itself in: wave model predicts brightness matters and
there should be a delay while energy accumulates. Neither happens.

**Rebuild the model (10 min).** Introduce photons: light arrives in discrete
packets of energy E = hf. One photon hits one electron. If that single packet
lacks enough energy, no amount of *additional* packets helps.

Analogy that works: you cannot jump a 3-metre wall by taking a thousand 1-metre
jumps. Also: intensity = how many photons, frequency = how much energy each one
carries. Those are independent, and only one of them matters for threshold.

**Verify quantitatively (10 min).** Students find the threshold frequency for a
given work function and check E = hf against the app's output. The numbers agree,
which makes the new model feel earned rather than asserted.

## Assessment
**Argument paragraph:** *"A student claims that any light will free electrons if
it's bright enough. Use evidence from your investigation to evaluate this claim."*

Rubric: states a clear position (2) · cites the below-threshold result at high
intensity (3) · cites the UV result at low intensity (2) · explains via photon
energy rather than restating the data (3).

## The bigger lesson
Ask at the close: **what does it take to abandon a theory that worked for a
century?** Not a preference. Not an authority. One reproducible experiment whose
result the theory cannot accommodate.

That's the most important thing in this lesson, and it isn't about light.

## Extension
Compare **🌊 Electron diffraction** with **⚾ Baseball matter wave**. If light can
behave as a particle, can matter behave as a wave? Yes — and the baseball's
wavelength is about 10⁻³⁴ m, which is why nobody notices. Quantum mechanics
doesn't switch off at human scale; it becomes unmeasurably small.
