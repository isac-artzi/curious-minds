# Twin Worlds, Different Fates

**Grades 9–12 · 50 minutes · 🪐 Planet / Exoplanet Builder**

**Standards:** HS-ESS1-4, HS-ESS2-4 · SEP 2, 6, 7 · CCC Stability and Change, Systems

## Objective
Students will explain the runaway greenhouse effect as a positive feedback loop
and evaluate which planetary variables determine long-term climate stability.

## The setup
Venus and Earth have nearly the same mass, radius, and composition, and formed
from the same material at the same time. One has oceans. One has a 460 °C surface
that melts lead. **Why?**

## Materials
- One device per student
- Feedback-loop diagram template (blank circular arrows)

## Procedure

**Present the puzzle (5 min).** Put the numbers up: Venus is 0.95 Earth radii,
0.82 Earth masses, 0.72 AU. Earth is 1.0, 1.0, 1.0. Ask for hypotheses before any
device is opened. Collect them on the board.

**Build both (10 min).** Students build Earth (1.0 AU, N₂–O₂, 70% water) and
Venus (0.72 AU, CO₂-dominant, 0% water) and compare outputs side by side.

**Isolate the variable (15 min).** The core question: *is it distance, or is it
atmosphere?* Test it properly:
1. Earth's atmosphere at Venus's distance (0.72 AU, N₂–O₂, 70% water)
2. Venus's atmosphere at Earth's distance (1.0 AU, CO₂, 0% water)

Neither reproduces real Venus exactly — and that's the finding. **It's both, and
they amplify each other.** Distance alone doesn't do it; atmosphere alone doesn't
do it.

**Draw the loop (10 min).** Students diagram the feedback:
> slightly warmer → more water evaporates → water vapor traps more heat → warmer
> still → more evaporation → … → oceans gone → carbon has nowhere to go → CO₂
> accumulates → hotter still

Have them mark where the loop *could* have been interrupted, and what would have
had to be different.

**Argue (10 min).** Position: *"Earth could never become Venus."* Students take a
side and defend it with evidence from their runs. Push on timescale, on the
carbonate–silicate cycle, on what "never" means.

## Assessment
**Feedback diagram + argument paragraph.** Diagram: four or more correctly
sequenced steps forming a closed loop (4). Paragraph: takes a clear position (2),
uses evidence from their own model runs (3), acknowledges the strongest
counterargument (1).

## Watch for this misconception
Students often think Venus is hot *because it's closer to the Sun*. Distance is
necessary but nowhere near sufficient — Mercury is closer still and much colder
at night. The atmosphere is the mechanism. Their own controlled runs prove it,
which is far better than being told.

## Extension
Use the **terraforming** controls: can you fix Venus? Which single change buys
the most? What would it cost, how long would it take, and who decides? Excellent
bridge into engineering constraints and ethics.
